Cultural practices often travel across communities, languages, and settings.
A video may show a familiar dance performed by a different community, a dish prepared with ingredients from another tradition, or a story adapted through another art form.
These examples require models to recognize cultural cues and understand how they relate.
We propose \benchmark{}, a benchmark for understanding cultural mixing in real-world video.
Each cue is described by its cultural associations, component type, and supporting visual, auditory, or textual evidence.
Two core tasks assess cue recognition and evidence-modality attribution.
A candidate task additionally asks models to identify a relation frame and assign its participants to semantic roles, while allowing symmetric relations and insufficient evidence.
The evaluation separates recognition errors from role-assignment errors and uses text-only controls to test what the video contributes.
