Cultural practices often travel across communities, languages, and settings.
A video may show a familiar dance performed by a different community, a dish prepared with ingredients from another tradition, or a story adapted through another art form.
Recognizing the elements separately does not establish whether a model understands their relationship.
We present the design of \benchmark{}, a benchmark for understanding cultural mixing in real-world video.
The proposed annotation represents cultural cues, their cultural associations, their component types, and the visual, auditory, or textual evidence supporting them.
Two core tasks assess cue recognition and evidence-modality attribution.
A candidate task additionally asks models to identify a relation frame and assign its participants to semantic roles, while allowing symmetric relations and insufficient evidence.
The planned evaluation separates recognition errors from role-assignment errors and tests dependence on cue descriptions and surrounding context.
This draft specifies the scope and evaluation design; it does not report completed annotation or model experiments.
