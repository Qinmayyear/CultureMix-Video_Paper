## Implications for the candidate culture-relation task

1. **Direction is a role convention, not historical flow.** A relation such as `performed_by(practice, performer)` orders two semantic roles. It does not show which culture influenced another or which culture owns a practice. Avoid a single universal “source culture -> target culture” field. Prefer a small inventory of relation frames with defined argument roles; serialize those roles in a fixed order only for scoring.
2. **Visible evidence and external evidence are different.** A visible lion-dance performance and its setting may support a relation. Performers' nationality, ethnicity, cultural membership, or migration history often do not. Do not infer these from appearance. Store cultural-association evidence and its source separately. If an answer requires supplied metadata, define a metadata-assisted track; do not claim that a video-only model should recover it.
3. **Typed input can leak the answer.** If a prompt supplies “Chinese lion dance” and “Nigerian troupe,” the likely roles may be solved from noun types alone. Include no-video and cue-text-only baselines. Use neutral cue IDs or grounded referents where possible. Construct plausible contrast sets with similar entity types and changed relations; implausible argument reversals test language plausibility instead of perception.
4. **Symmetric coexistence is not synthesis.** Co-presence is symmetric and can be supported by simultaneous evidence. Synthesis or incorporation needs an explicit criterion, such as contributions to the same identifiable practice, object, or event. Camera editing, a soundtrack, or neighboring objects alone must not silently be treated as transformed cultural practice. Allow `insufficient evidence` and annotate why.
5. **Relations need not be mutually exclusive.** One event can have a culturally associated practice, performers, costume, soundtrack, and local setting. A pair or event can satisfy several relations. Prefer atomic relation questions or a set of frame instances over forcing one video-level category.
6. **Cultural association is not necessarily exclusive.** Shared, diasporic, and transnational practices can map to several communities. Distinct country strings are not sufficient evidence of distinct traditions. Permit multiple supported associations and record ambiguity.
7. **Culture understanding must exceed generic scene graphs.** Correctly identifying “performed_by” can require little cultural knowledge. Report culture-association binding separately from generic relation/role accuracy, and require grounded evidence connecting culturally meaningful cues. Otherwise the benchmark risks measuring ordinary action/role recognition under cultural labels.
8. **Conservative novelty.** Directed visual relations and role labeling are established. A defensible claim is that CulMix-Video investigates how these representations can evaluate relations among culturally associated elements in video. The cultural relation inventory, annotation reliability, and answerability still need a pilot.

## Suggested conservative text

The proposed relation task combines cultural-association labels with observable event or scene roles. It does not infer historical borrowing, cultural ownership, or participants' identities from visual appearance. Direction denotes the ordered roles of a specified relation, not a direction of cultural influence. The relation inventory remains a candidate design until independent annotation demonstrates that its labels can be applied consistently.
